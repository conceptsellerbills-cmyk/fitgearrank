## The Problem With Cleansers Designed for Dry Skin

Dry skin presents a fundamental cleansing paradox: you need to remove impurities, sunscreen, and surface debris from the skin daily, but most cleansing agents — surfactants — work by stripping oils from surfaces, including the skin's own natural lipid barrier. For normal or oily skin types, this trade-off is manageable. For dry to very dry skin, standard cleansers cause net harm: they remove the very oils the skin is already struggling to produce, leaving the barrier compromised, skin tight and flaky, and subsequent moisturizers working to repair damage rather than build on a healthy foundation.

The solutions most people reach for — cream cleansers, milk cleansers, micellar waters — are gentler but often sacrifice cleansing efficacy. They leave sunscreen residue, don't fully emulsify makeup, and can leave a film that blocks active ingredients in serums and treatments.

It cleans thoroughly without stripping, and it leaves skin in better condition than before cleansing rather than worse. Most cleansers are either water-based (gels, foams, micellar) or oil-based (cleansing oils, balms). Each format has limitations:

**Water-based cleansers** use surfactants to emulsify oils and rinse them away — effective at cleansing but potentially stripping, especially for dry skin types.

**Oil-based cleansers** dissolve makeup and sunscreen beautifully through the "like dissolves like" principle, but many leave a residue and don't fully rinse clean without a second cleanse.

The **foaming oil cleanser** format used here is an oil-in-water emulsion: oil droplets suspended in water with emulsifying agents that allow the formula to lather, rinse cleanly, and leave no residue — while the oils themselves do their cleansing and moisturizing work during the contact time before rinsing.

The result is a product that:
- Lathers and rinses like a traditional cleanser (no oily film left behind)
- Dissolves oil-based impurities, sunscreen, and makeup without requiring a dedicated oil cleansing step
- Delivers moisturizing actives to the skin during the cleansing step
- Leaves skin feeling clean, soft, and hydrated — not tight, stripped, or dry

This format was developed specifically in response to the needs of dry skin types who found that standard cleansers worsened their skin condition and that oil cleansers left residue they were uncomfortable with.

## Key Ingredients: What Each One Does

**Squalane:**
Squalane is a stabilized form of squalene — a lipid naturally found in human sebum at high concentrations in younger skin that declines significantly with age. As sebum squalene levels drop, skin becomes drier, less supple, and more prone to fine lines. Topical squalane replenishes this deficit directly.

Squalane is an exceptionally well-tolerated emollient: it's lightweight, non-comedogenic (doesn't clog pores), penetrates the skin barrier efficiently, and is compatible with all skin types including sensitive and acne-prone. In the context of a cleanser for dry skin, squalane provides the key function of replacing the lipids that the cleansing surfactants remove — so the net effect on skin barrier moisture is neutral to positive rather than negative.

**Hyaluronic Acid (HA):**
As a humectant, hyaluronic acid attracts and binds water molecules from the environment and the deeper layers of the skin to the surface tissue. In a rinse-off cleanser, the contact time is brief, but HA still delivers a meaningful hydration boost that contributes to the "clean but not tight" sensation after rinsing. HA is also a key signaling molecule for skin repair — its presence in the formula supports the skin's own recovery processes.

**Ceramides (Ceramide NP, Ceramide AP, Ceramide EOP):**
Ceramides are the primary structural lipids in the skin's outer barrier — the stratum corneum. They form the "mortar" between skin cells (corneocytes), holding them together in a tight, moisture-retaining matrix. Dry skin is almost always characterized by depleted ceramide levels in the barrier, which is why it loses moisture rapidly and becomes rough and flaky.

CeraVe's formulas consistently include multiple ceramide types rather than a single variety, because the skin's natural ceramide profile is diverse — different ceramide subtypes perform different structural functions. Ceramide NP, AP, and EOP together replicate the natural lipid mixture more accurately than a single ceramide ingredient, providing more complete barrier support.

**MVE Technology (Multivesicular Emulsion):**
CeraVe's patented delivery system encapsulates the ceramides in a layered vesicle structure that releases them gradually over time rather than all at once. Even in a rinse-off cleanser, the MVE technology begins delivering ceramides to the skin barrier during the cleansing step, with continued release into the skin after rinsing. This extends the effective treatment window beyond what contact time alone would allow.

Fragrance is the most common cause of allergic contact dermatitis from skincare products, and dry skin is inherently more susceptible to irritant reactions because a compromised barrier allows allergens and irritants to penetrate more easily. For dry, sensitive, or eczema-prone skin, fragrance in a daily-use cleanser represents a recurring irritation source that can chronically worsen the very condition the cleanser is meant to address.

CeraVe's fragrance-free commitment across their product line is one of the reasons dermatologists recommend the brand consistently for sensitive and reactive skin. The Foaming Oil Cleanser has no masking fragrance, no "natural" essential oil fragrance (which is equally sensitizing), and no artificial scent — just the formula itself, which is odor-neutral.

**Residue-free** addresses the key practical concern about oil-based and oil-containing cleansers: the film they leave. Many cleansing oils and balms require a rinse with a separate water-based cleanser to remove the residue, negating the simplicity of a single-cleanse routine. The emulsion technology in the Foaming Oil Cleanser ensures that it rinses completely clean with water alone — no second cleanse required, no tacky or greasy feeling on the skin post-rinse, and no interference with active ingredients applied in subsequent steps.

## How to Use for Best Results

**As a face cleanser (morning):**
Wet face with lukewarm water. Pump a small amount (one to two pumps) into hands, work into a lather, and massage across the face for 30–60 seconds. Rinse thoroughly. Follow immediately with toner (if used), serum, and moisturizer with SPF. Morning use removes overnight oils and preps skin for daytime active ingredients.

**As a face cleanser (evening — single cleanse):**
If you wear minimal makeup or no makeup, a single cleanse with the Foaming Oil Cleanser removes the day's sebum, sunscreen residue, and environmental pollutants completely. Massage for 60 seconds to allow the squalane to fully emulsify oil-based residue, then rinse.

**As a face cleanser (evening — double cleanse):**
For heavy makeup, long-wear foundation, or high-SPF sunscreen, use a dedicated cleansing oil or micellar water as the first cleanse to break down product buildup, then follow with the CeraVe Foaming Oil Cleanser as the second cleanse to complete the process and deliver barrier actives.

**As a body wash:**
Apply directly to wet skin in the shower, lather across dry areas (legs, arms, torso), and rinse. The larger 19 fl oz bottle supports body use without depleting the supply too quickly. Follow immediately with body moisturizer while skin is still slightly damp to maximize hydration retention.
