## Why Women Need Protein Powder

Protein is the building block of muscle — and getting enough from food alone is harder than most people realize. For active women, the recommended protein intake is 0.7-1g per pound of body weight, depending on training intensity.

A 140-pound woman doing strength training needs 100-140g of protein per day. That's roughly 4-5 chicken breasts. Protein powder makes hitting that target practical without eating an exhausting amount of food.

Beyond muscle building, adequate protein supports:
- Fat loss (protein has the highest thermic effect of any macronutrient)
- Hunger control (protein is the most satiating macro)
- Bone density (important for women's long-term health)
- Recovery from exercise

## What to Look for in Protein Powder

**Protein content**: Aim for at least 20-25g per serving. Check the serving size — some brands use large serving sizes to inflate the number.

**Leucine content**: Leucine is the amino acid that triggers muscle protein synthesis. Look for 2-3g per serving.

**Ingredient quality**: Minimal ingredients, no proprietary blends (which hide individual amounts), no excessive artificial sweeteners.

**Third-party testing**: NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport certification means the product has been tested for banned substances and label accuracy.

**Taste**: You'll drink this daily. It has to taste good.

## Collagen Protein: Worth It?

Collagen protein has surged in popularity — especially for women concerned about skin, hair, nails, and joint health.

**What the science says:**
- Collagen does support joint health and has clinical evidence behind it
- It does NOT build muscle effectively (low leucine content)
- Skin benefits are real but modest in clinical trials

**Best use**: Add collagen to coffee or a smoothie alongside a whey or plant-based protein for muscle building. Don't rely on it as your primary protein source.

**Best collagen**: Vital Proteins Collagen Peptides (~$25-30 for 10 oz). Unflavored, dissolves in any liquid.

## How to Use Protein Powder

**Post-workout**: Within 2 hours of strength training is the most research-supported timing.

**Breakfast add-in**: Add a scoop to oatmeal, yogurt, or smoothies to hit protein targets early in the day.

**Snack**: Protein shake as a snack is legitimate — it's satiating and nutrient-dense.

**Daily target**: Use food for most of your protein. Use powder to close the gap. Aim for whole food sources first.

## Ingredients to Avoid

**Proprietary blends**: When multiple ingredients are listed under a "blend" with only a total weight, you can't know how much of each ingredient you're getting.

**Excessive artificial sweeteners**: A small amount is fine. Large amounts of sucralose can disrupt gut microbiome in some people.

**Heavy metal contamination**: Some plant-based proteins test high for heavy metals (lead, cadmium from soil). Look for third-party tested brands — this is where certification matters most for plant proteins.
